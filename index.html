Fundamentals

Six ideas that explain most of it

If you understand these, you can ignore about 90% of what crypto marketing says — because you can check the claims yourself.

₿

A ledger, not a bank

Bitcoin is a shared record of who owns what, copied onto thousands of independent computers. No single company or government holds the master copy, so there is nothing central to switch off or edit.

◎

Blocks and the chain

Transactions are bundled into blocks. Each block carries a cryptographic fingerprint of the one before it, so rewriting history would mean redoing all the work since then — which is why old records are practically permanent.

⛏

Mining is bookkeeping with a cost

Miners compete to add the next block by burning real electricity on computation. They are paid in new coins plus fees. The cost is the point: it makes faking a block expensive instead of free.

🔑

Keys, not accounts

Your "balance" is an entry anyone can verify. Spending it requires a private key only you should have. Lose the key and the coins stay on the ledger forever, unreachable by anyone — including you.

21,000,000

A hard supply cap

The protocol will never issue more than about 21 million bitcoin — precisely 20,999,999.9769, because the last satoshi-level rounding cuts it just short. Every full node rejects any block that breaks that rule.

◐

Halving, every four years

New coins arrive as a mining reward that halves every 210,000 blocks. Bitcoin's annual issuance fell below 1% after the April 2024 halving and will drop under 0.5% around 2028.

The supply engine

Why the issuance curve matters more than the price chart

Bitcoin's monetary policy was fixed in 2009 and has never been changed. Each halving cuts the flow of new coins in half, while the stock of existing coins keeps growing — so the ratio of new supply to old supply falls every four years.

    Before you buy

    Four risks that survive every bull market

    Crypto is a young, volatile, lightly regulated asset class where irreversible mistakes are normal. None of these problems get solved by being early.

    1

    Volatility is not a bug

    Total crypto market value has swung from roughly $2.1 trillion to $3.0 trillion within a single quarter, and bitcoin dominance has ranged from about 56% to 60% this year. Position sizes should assume a 50–80% drawdown is possible.

    2

    Self-custody cuts both ways

    Holding your own keys removes counterparty risk and adds personal risk: a lost seed phrase, a phishing site, or a malware-infested browser can wipe a balance with no chargeback and no support line.

    3

    Most tokens are not bitcoin

    The top asset has a fixed, verifiable supply and fifteen years of uptime. A new coin may have an unlimited supply, an upgradeable contract that can be changed by its team, or a token whose main product is the token itself.

    4

    Yield usually means someone else's risk

    Lending platforms, "staking rewards" and algorithmic stablecoins have repeatedly failed in ways depositors did not understand. If you cannot name where the return comes from, you are probably the return.

    Glossary

    Plain-language definitions

    Satoshi
    The smallest unit of bitcoin: 0.00000001 BTC. Named after Bitcoin's pseudonymous creator, whose identity is still unknown.
    Private key
    A long secret number that proves ownership. Whoever holds it controls the coins; there is no password reset.
    Seed phrase
    Twelve or twenty-four words that regenerate a whole wallet. Treat it like the deed to a house — never type it into a website.
    Cold storage
    Keeping keys on a device that never touches the internet, so remote attackers have nothing to reach.
    Dominance
    One asset's market cap divided by the total crypto market cap. It measures relative size, not quality.
    Stablecoin
    A token pegged to a currency, usually the dollar. The peg depends entirely on the issuer's reserves and is only as solid as they are.
    Gas fee
    A payment for the computation a network must do to process your transaction. Fees rise when the network is busy.
    Proof of stake
    An alternative to mining where validators are chosen in proportion to coins locked up. It trades electricity for capital at risk.
    Approval
    Permission you grant a contract to move your tokens. Unlimited approvals are the single most exploited permission in crypto.
    Drainer
    Malicious software or a fake site that tricks you into signing away your funds, usually in one irreversible click.
